What It Actually Is

Not a hatch. A hunt.

Most people who fish Michigan at night are hex fishing — waiting on a mayfly, casting to rising trout for a few weeks in early summer. That is a wonderful thing and it is not what this trip is.

Mousing is searching the river with a big rat. You swing and skate a deer-hair mouse across likely water — undercut banks, log jams, the seam at the head of a pool — and you wait for something enormous to try to kill it. The take is not a sip. It is a boil, or a wallop, or the sound of a cinder block hitting the water four feet from your knees.

The fish that do this are not the fish you see in the daytime. They are the largest, most predacious fish in the whole system, the ones that spend daylight hours under wood and come out to feed when the river belongs to them.

Mousing with a big rat can be a lot like streamer fishing, in the fact that you may only have a few encounters in the whole night — but the ones that you will have will be quality.

That is the honest pitch. This is not a numbers trip. If you want a bent rod all day, book a summer dry fly float and we will have a great time. If you want one shot at the biggest brown trout of your life, go at night.

The Shape of the Night

8 PM to 3 AM.

Start times move with the calendar — earlier in late summer, later around the solstice. Here is roughly how a night runs.

8–10 PM

Meet at the river as the light dies. Rig up, walk in, and learn the water while you can still see it — where the wood is, where the bank undercuts, where you are going to stand three hours from now.

11 PM

Full dark. Lights go off the water. The big fish that hid all day start to move out of the wood and hunt the shallows. So do we.

1 AM

Prime time. Working the best runs with the mouse, slow and deliberate. Every cast counts now. This is usually when it happens.

3 AM

Walk out under the stars, hopefully with a photograph you will never have to exaggerate.

Mousing FAQ

What people ask before their first night.

Do I need to be a good caster to go mousing?

No. Mousing is close-quarters, short-line fishing — you are not throwing eighty feet in the dark. If you can put a heavy fly thirty feet and swing it under control, you can do this. I will spend the first hour of light getting your stroke where it needs to be.

How many fish will we catch?

Mousing is like streamer fishing: you may only have a few encounters in the whole night, but the ones you have will be quality. Some nights produce several fish; some produce one you will remember for years. I do not sell numbers on this trip.

What is the best month for a mousing trip?

Summer, broadly — the warm months when trout feed hard after dark. Dates go quickly around the peak, so book ahead. Call or text and I will tell you honestly what the river is doing.

Is this the same thing as hex fishing?

No. Hex fishing is a hatch — you are fishing a mayfly to rising trout for a few weeks a year. Mousing is hunting. You are working a big rodent pattern through prime lies looking for the largest, most predacious fish in the system, and it is not tied to a hatch window.

What do I need to bring at night?

Safety or eye glasses — required, because a big deer-hair mouse moving in the dark is no joke — plus layered clothing, bug spray, and waders or muck boots. Bring a headlamp if you have one, though we keep light off the water as much as possible. Rods, reels, mice, leaders and tackle are included.

Can we bring a third angler?

The night trip is priced for up to two anglers. Night fishing is close work in the dark, and two is the number that fishes well. Ask me if you have a third.
